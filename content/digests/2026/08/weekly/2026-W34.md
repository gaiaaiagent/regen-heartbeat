## Ecocredit Trends

Ecocredits remain the core instrument for translating on-the-ground regeneration into verifiable, tradeable ecological assets. Recent activity includes guides for retiring ecocredits on behalf of third parties, transferring credits between wallets, creating sell orders on the marketplace, and putting credits into baskets for composable ecological portfolios.

From the broader regenerative agriculture carbon market, AgreenaCarbon delivered 2.3 million Verra-verified carbon credits in May 2026, demonstrating that regenerative agriculture projects can achieve meaningful scale. Ecometric has completed the four-stage US Carbon Registry Regen Network peer review process and been allocated its own credit class on the Regen Registry. These developments signal maturation — from pilot projects to production-scale issuance.

The Terrasos Biodiversity Units (TBU) launch on Regen Network represents a critical expansion beyond carbon into multi-dimensional ecological value. This aligns with the global biodiversity credits market, which was valued at approximately $89.65 million in 2025 and is projected to reach $948.25 million by 2034, growing at a CAGR of 29.96%. Unlike carbon credits, which primarily focus on greenhouse gas reductions, biodiversity credits emphasize ecological restoration — restoring natural habitats, strengthening resilience, protecting threatened species, and reconnecting ecological corridors.

## Forward Look

Several threads merit attention in the coming weeks. The biodiversity credit market is entering policy formalization — at least 56 countries including Australia, Brazil, Canada, China, Colombia, France, Germany, India, Mexico, New Zealand, and South Africa now have laws or policies requiring biodiversity offsets or compensatory conservation measures. Vietnam plans to pilot biodiversity credits as part of its strategy to diversify financial resources for nature conservation and restoration.

A development bank is moving ahead with plans for a nature credit framework linked to the Amazon's atmospheric moisture flows, seeking consultancy for valuation and credit-design work. This represents a frontier in ecological accounting — crediting not just carbon sequestration or biodiversity, but hydrological services at bioregional scale.

Within Regen Network, upcoming governance proposals will likely address tokenomics evolution, methodological approvals for new credit types, and infrastructure decisions around DAO tooling and cross-chain bridges. The weekly digest from KOI references community discussions about the role Regen Registry plays in ensuring community-governed credit standards drive carbon market integrity — an ongoing conversation about quality, verification, and the balance between permissionless innovation and rigorous standards.

The Cosmos ecosystem's 2026 roadmap includes finalizing IBC bridges to Solana and Base, enterprise blockchain management tools, and a governance-driven tokenomics overhaul. These developments create new capabilities for Regen Network to leverage as it scales ecological credit issuance and marketplace activity.

What remains open is how the ecosystem will navigate the tension between rapid growth (biodiversity credits scaling 10x by 2034) and maintaining the verification rigor and community governance that distinguish Regen from conventional carbon offset markets. The next weeks will clarify whether the Registry 2.0 model can accommodate both imperatives — innovation and integrity — without compromising either.
