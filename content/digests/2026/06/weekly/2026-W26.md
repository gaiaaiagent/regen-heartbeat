## Forward Look

June closes with the climate finance community at a documented inflection point. The OECD's June 9 release of its review on aligning finance with climate goals underscores a shift: climate finance is entering a multipolar era, less driven by multilateral commitments and more by geopolitics, energy security, and mounting impacts. For Regen Network, this broader trend matters. Ecological credits are a financial instrument. Their uptake depends not just on methodology quality but on where capital is flowing and what mandates are shaping demand.

The World Resources Institute identifies six opportunities for sustainable finance in 2026, and UNEP's State of Finance for Nature 2026 report quantifies the gap: nature-based solutions investment must increase 2.5 times to $571 billion annually by 2030 to meet biodiversity, climate, and land restoration targets. Regen's infrastructure sits downstream of these capital flows. If climate finance begins to route through on-chain rails, Regen is positioned. If it doesn't, the network's technological elegance becomes academic.

In the Cosmos ecosystem, June 11 brought news of infrastructure consolidation: Cosmos Labs acquired the Mintscan product suite, centralizing the block explorer, Skip:Go, and IBC Eureka under one maintainer. This is relevant for Regen as a Cosmos chain — better infrastructure for IBC means better interoperability, which means easier on-ramps and off-ramps for ecocredits moving between chains.

The questions entering July: Will the tokenomics working group proposals move to governance votes? Will new credit classes enter peer review? Will marketplace activity — the missing data point this week — show growth or stagnation? The knowledge base is rich with structure. What it cannot yet show is momentum.
